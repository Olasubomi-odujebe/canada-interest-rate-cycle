# Canada's Interest Rate Cycle: How Monetary Policy Responded to Rising Inflation

**September 2016 – July 2026**  
**Author: Olasubomi P. Odujebe**

## Overview

This project examines Canada's monetary policy cycle between September 2016 and July 2026, focusing on how the Bank of Canada's policy interest rate changed as inflation moved through different stages.

The analysis looks at the relationship between inflation and the policy interest rate, major turning points, and changes across different economic periods.

## Research Question

**How did monetary policy respond to rising inflation in Canada between September 2016 and July 2026?**

## Key Findings

- The policy interest rate fell to **0.25%** during the pandemic.
- Inflation reached a peak of **8.13%**.
- The policy interest rate later reached a peak of **5.00%**.
- The inflation and policy-rate peaks occurred **13 months apart**.
- By July 2026, inflation had declined to **3.03%**, while the policy interest rate stood at **2.25%**.
- The analysis is descriptive and does not establish a causal relationship between monetary policy and inflation.

## Analytical Approach

The analysis examines:

1. Policy interest rate changes over time
2. Inflation and policy-rate peaks
3. The timing difference between the two peaks
4. Changes following the peaks
5. Average inflation and policy rates across five economic periods

The five periods examined are:

- Pre-pandemic
- Pandemic & Recovery
- Inflation Shock & Tightening
- Easing
- Stabilization

## Data Sources

The analysis uses Canadian economic data on:

- Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation
- Bank of Canada's target for the overnight policy rate

## Limitations

This analysis is descriptive rather than causal. It identifies patterns and timing between inflation and the policy interest rate but does not conclude that changes in the policy rate alone caused changes in inflation.

Other factors, including supply conditions, energy prices, labour-market conditions, exchange rates, and consumer demand, can also influence inflation.
